Concept iOS app · Singapore

A teman for the cancer journey.

Teman means "companion" in Malay. The app would help people with cancer in Singapore, and the people who care for them, to understand what they are told, find the right care, get to appointments, and walk into every consultation with the right questions.

Read the Brainstorm View on GitHub

Status Design and architecture only. No app has shipped, and nothing here is medical advice.

Every promise is enforced in the design.

Every boundary here is built into the product, and none of them is fine print. The model is never the source of truth, and the patient's medical team stays in charge.

Teman never…
Instead, Teman…
Diagnoses cancer, or reads a result as good or bad
Explains what the word means, cites the source, and suggests the question to ask
Chooses, compares or ranks treatments
Explains how a treatment the doctor has named generally works, and helps prepare questions
Determines clinical-trial eligibility
Explains what trials are and how to ask the oncologist about one
Replaces the medical team, or decides urgency
Shows the patient's own care-team instructions and 995, using fixed rules rather than AI
Says anything medical without a source
Attaches a named Singapore source and a "last checked" date to every medical sentence
Shares patient information
Keeps everything on the iPhone, with no account, analytics or server. Data leaves only when the patient exports it

Built around what patients and caregivers say.

Three things patients and caregivers say, and the features that answer them.

"You have to be a huge patient advocate. Push for answers. Push for biopsies. Push for proper testing. Ask questions."

Ask: a question builder seeded from NCCS and SCS lists, "questions about tests", and a visit-mode checklist so nothing is left unanswered.

"I need help navigating difficult conversations and advocating for myself."

Conversations: private rehearsal and scripts for the doctor, family, children and an employer, a "What matters to me" card, and words for asking for a second opinion.

"The volume of doctors, documents, and insurance paperwork can quickly become overwhelming."

Binder and Money: scan a letter and the app reads it on-device and files it, a "who's who" care team, and plain-language MediShield Life, Cancer Drug List and MediFund cards.

Seven stages, one companion.

Teman meets people where they are, and shows only what fits that moment. Content about palliative care stays hidden until the patient chooses to open it.

  1. Just diagnosedWords I heard, scan my report, the first week
  2. Care teamWho is who, who to call, the Medical Social Worker
  3. Planning treatmentQuestion builder, visit mode, second opinion
  4. Paying for careMediShield Life, CDL, MediSave, MediFund, charity funds
  5. During treatmentReminders, getting there, side effects, when to call
  6. Mind and familyCheck-in, psychosocial care, caregivers, conversations
  7. What's nextSurvivorship, or palliative care offered early and gently

Designed with Apple's Human Interface Guidelines.

A standard five-tab structure, text that scales to the largest accessibility sizes, 60-point targets for hands affected by neuropathy, and Lock Screen notices that never name a diagnosis. Read the design doc.

Accessibility first

Dynamic Type to AX5, VoiceOver labels on every control, Reduce Motion, and meaning never carried by colour alone.

Ask in context

No permission prompts at launch. Each permission is requested when its feature is first used, with a one-sentence reason.

Honest notifications

Time Sensitive only for "leave now". Medicines use AlarmKit. Teman sends no marketing.

Four languages

English, Chinese, Malay and Tamil, with large text and read-aloud for tired eyes.

Native iOS, for holistic care.

Apple's own frameworks run on the device, are accessible by default, and work offline. Full framework map.

On-device AI that reads, rewrites and drafts, and never decides.

Local language models (Apple's Foundation Models by default, with optional open-weight packs such as a phone-sized SEA-LION for Southeast Asian languages) run entirely on the iPhone. Teman's design assumes a small model will sometimes be wrong, so the model never acts as a source of truth. Read the LLM brainstorm.

Red-flag gate. Fixed rules catch fever, bleeding or breathlessness and show the care team's instructions and 995.
Scope gate. Requests to diagnose, choose treatment, check eligibility or give prognosis go to "Ask your doctor".
Retrieve. Search only the bundled, reviewed Singapore source library.
Generate. Guided generation: every sentence must name its source.
Verify. Drop uncited, unsupported or interpretive sentences.
Show. Citation chips, "Drafted on this iPhone", and Edit, Retry or Discard.

Explain a word

A Primary 6-level definition, built only from cited passages.

Read my letter

Extracts the date, time, place and preparation, and you confirm before saving.

Suggest questions

Picks from a curated question library and rewords for you. It never invents clinical questions.

Summarise my visit

A draft from your own on-device recording, with quotes, for you to check.

Nothing to leak.

The safest patient data is data the publisher never holds. Safety and privacy doc.

No account, no server

Teman has no sign-up, analytics or third-party SDKs. Its App Store label would read "Data Not Collected".

Encrypted on the iPhone

Complete file protection, CryptoKit-encrypted documents, and an optional Face ID lock.

You choose what to share

Exports open in the share sheet with a preview. Teman points to HealthHub Caregiver Access for official record sharing.

Trusted Singapore sources, always cited.

Medical and financial facts come from Singapore public bodies and national cancer centres first, then established Singapore charities. International sources are used for definitions only, and are labelled. Full source registry.

In an emergency, call 995. Teman is a concept, not a medical service. If you are on cancer treatment and feel unwell, call the number your care team gave you. For support, the NCCS Cancer Helpline and the Singapore Cancer Society can help.
